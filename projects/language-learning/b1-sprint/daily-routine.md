# Daily & Weekly Routine

Spanish is **morning**. French is **afternoon/evening**. This never changes — it's the main defence against mixing them up.

---

## The six blocks

Every day is assembled from these. Durations change by phase; the blocks don't.

| Block | When | Language | Type | What |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Machine** | First thing, before phone | Spanish | Active | Anki ES, then grammar/Language Transfer |
| **B — Transit In** | Commute / walk / gym | Spanish | Stacked | Podcast or CI audio |
| **C — Midday Hit** | Lunch break | French | Semi-active | Anki FR + *Journal en français facile* |
| **D — Transit Out** | Commute home / cooking | French | Stacked | Podcast or CI audio |
| **E — Desk** | Evening | French | Active | Reading, writing, Kwiziq |
| **F — Mouth** | Last thing | Alternates | Active | Shadowing / monologue |

**Block F alternates:** Spanish on odd calendar days, French on even. Say it out loud, standing up, no notes. 15 minutes.

## Template 3 — Bar Shift / Minimum Viable (60 min)
*Any day that's gone wrong. Never skip this — the streak is the asset.*

- Anki ES — 10 min
- Spanish audio — 20 min (bar prep, the drive, the walk home)
- Anki FR — 10 min
- French audio — 20 min

That's it. No guilt, no catch-up debt. Just don't let two land back to back.

| Day | Extra on top of the daily template |
|---|---|
| **Mon** | — |
| **Tue** | Spanish tutor, 45 min |
| **Wed** | French tutor, 45 min |
| **Thu** | — |
| **Fri** | Spanish tutor, 45 min |
| **Sat** | French tutor, 45 min · **Film night** — one film or 2 eps of TV in the week's language, target-language subs |
| **Sun** | **Writing hour** — one piece per language, submitted for correction · **Weekly review** (20 min): log hours, update `progress/`, pick next week's episodes |

**Tutor cadence by phase:**
- Phase 0 (Sept): none. Build the machinery first.
- Phase 1 (Oct–Nov): 1× Spanish/wk from week 5. Add 1× French/wk from week 9.
- Phase 2 (Dec–Feb): 2× Spanish + 2× French per week. **This is the big lift.**
- Phase 3–4 (Mar–May): 2× each, shifting to exam-format practice from April.

## Sunday writing hour — what to write

| Phase | Per language | Corrected by |
|---|---|---|
| Sept–Oct | 3 sentences/day in a notebook. No correction. Just build the habit. | — |
| Nov | 100 words/week — describe your week | LangCorrect (free) |
| Dec–Feb | 2× 150–200 words/week — opinion, story, letter | Tutor, first 10 min of a session |
| Mar–Apr | 1× 160–180 words/week, **timed 45 min**, DELF/DELE format | Tutor |
| May | 2× timed exam papers/week | Tutor |

Writing is the highest-leverage thing most learners skip, because speaking lets you dodge every structure you don't know. Writing doesn't.

## Reading ladder

Reading is the cheapest vocabulary growth available. Start early, ignore the purists.

| Phase | Spanish | French | Volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sept | Podcast transcripts only, read *while* listening | Same | 10 min/day |
| Oct–Nov | *Short Stories in Spanish for Beginners* (Olly Richards) | *Short Stories in French for Beginners* | 1 story/week |
| Dec–Jan | Finish beginner book → start **Intermediate** collection | Same | 20 min/day |
| Feb–Mar | First real book: a YA novel or *El Principito* | *Le Petit Prince* | 20 pages/wk |
| Apr–May | News + DELE/DELF reading papers | *L'Étranger* (Camus — deceptively simple prose) + DELF papers | 20 min/day |

**Target: ~4 books per language across the nine months.**

**How to read:** first pass, no dictionary, tolerate not understanding. Second pass, look up only words that blocked meaning. Mine 5 sentences into Anki. Never look up every word — you'll quit by week three.

## Two drills worth doing properly

**Shadowing (Block F).** Play 60 seconds of audio. Play it again and speak *along with it*, matching rhythm and speed, not waiting for gaps. Repeat that same 60 seconds 5–6 times. It's the fastest fix for pronunciation and it forces your mouth to move at native speed.

**Dictation — French only, 2×/week, 10 min.** Play a 30-second clip. Write down exactly what you hear. Check against the transcript. This is the specific cure for the French listening wall: it exposes that you can't hear word boundaries, which reading French will never reveal.
