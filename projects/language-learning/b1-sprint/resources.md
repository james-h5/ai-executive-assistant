# The Resource Stack

Everything below is either free or cheap. **Tier 1 = non-negotiable core.** Tier 2 = rotation, pick what you enjoy. Tier 3 = optional.

Channel handles change; if a link 404s, search the name — the resource almost certainly still exists.

# 🇪🇸 SPANISH

## Tier 1 — Core

### Language Transfer — Complete Spanish
**[languagetransfer.org](https://www.languagetransfer.org/complete-spanish)** · free app, YouTube, podcast · **90 lessons, ~17 hrs**
The single best starting point in existence, and it's free with no ads. Audio-only "Thinking Method" — a teacher walks a real student through building Spanish from English cognates and logic. You'll construct sentences you were never taught.
**When:** Sept–Oct, Block A. Two lessons a day. Finish it.
**How:** Actually pause and answer out loud. Passive listening wastes it entirely.

### Dreaming Spanish
**[dreamingspanish.com](https://www.dreaming.com)** · YouTube free tier is large · ~$14/mo full library
The best comprehensible input library for any language. Tiered Superbeginner → Beginner → Intermediate → Advanced, with real-time drawings and gesture at the low levels so you understand from day one without translation.
**When:** Sept → Feb, daily, escalating 20 → 60 min.
**Ignore:** their advice to stay silent for 600 hours. Wrong goal for this timeline (see README §2).

### Anki
**[apps.ankiweb.net](https://apps.ankiweb.net)** · free (desktop/Android), ~$25 iOS
Spaced repetition. Non-negotiable — it's the only thing stopping vocabulary leaking back out.
**Setup:** Start with a Spanish frequency top-1000/5000 deck. From December, most new cards should be **your own sentence mines** — sentences pulled from things you actually watched, with one unknown word each.
**Dose:** 15–20 new/day Sept–Jan, drop to 10/day Feb onward.

### italki
**[italki.com](https://www.italki.com)** · community tutors ~$8–12/hr
Where B1 actually gets built. **Community** tutors (not professional teachers) for conversation — cheaper and better suited to what you need.
**When:** From ~5 Oct. 1×/wk → 2×/wk from December.
**How:** Book two at a time so there's always a next one. Tell them from session one: *"I'm sitting DELE B1 in May, correct me aggressively."*

### Short Stories in Spanish — Olly Richards (Teach Yourself)
**Beginners** (A2–B1) → **Intermediate Learners** (B1–B2). ~$25 each.
Purpose-built graded readers with glossaries and comprehension checks. The beginner collection from October, intermediate from December.

# 🇫🇷 FRENCH

> French needs more listening hours than Spanish. Spoken French drops syllables, links words, and swallows endings — you will read it long before you hear it. Every French recommendation below is weighted toward the ear.

## Tier 1 — Core

### Week 1: Pronunciation boot camp
**Do this before anything else.** 20 min/day × 5 days.
- **Comme une Française** (YouTube, Géraldine) — pronunciation and real spoken French
- **Français avec Pierre** ([francaisavecpierre.com](https://www.francaisavecpierre.com)) — pronunciation playlist
**Cover:** nasal vowels (*on/an/in/un*), liaison, elision, the French `r`, and silent endings.
Skipping this costs you months later. It is the highest-return week in the entire plan.

### Language Transfer — Introduction to French
**[languagetransfer.org](https://www.languagetransfer.org)** · free · **40 lessons, ~5 hrs**
Same method as Spanish but much shorter — it's an *introduction*, not a complete course. Finish it by ~10 Oct, then hand over to Assimil.

### Assimil — French With Ease
~$45 book + audio · ~100 lessons
Picks up the structural slack Language Transfer French leaves behind. One 20-minute lesson a day, October → January. Graded, relentless, slightly dated, works.

### Kwiziq French
**[french.kwiziq.com](https://french.kwiziq.com)** · ~$20/mo
The best CEFR-mapped adaptive grammar tool for any language, and it's built around DELF levels. Placement test in October, then 15 kwizzes a day. **This is the one paid tool I'd insist on.**

### InnerFrench
**[innerfrench.com](https://innerfrench.com)** · YouTube + podcast · **free, with free transcripts**
🔴 **The centrepiece of the French plan.** Hugo speaks slowly and clearly at real B1 level about interesting adult topics — psychology, history, French culture. Free interactive transcripts.
**When:** From December, 3 episodes/week, each **twice**: once cold, once with the transcript.
If you only kept one French resource, this is it.

### Journal en français facile — RFI
**[rfi.fr](https://www.rfi.fr/fr/podcasts/journal-en-fran%C3%A7ais-facile)** · free, daily, ~10 min, free transcripts
Daily world news in deliberately slow, clear French. **Start week 3 of September and never stop.** Ten minutes a day, every day, for nine months. Early on you'll catch nothing — it's calibration. By February it'll be your comprehension yardstick.

# What to deliberately avoid

- **Duolingo.** Gamified, slow, teaches you to recognise rather than produce. At 19 hrs/week it's a rounding error that eats the time your best resources need. Delete it.
- **Grammar textbooks read cover to cover.** Grammar is for answering questions the input raised, not for front-loading.
- **Any new app after October.** Resource-hopping is procrastination that feels like diligence.
- **English subtitles.** From January, target-language subs or none. English subs mean you're reading a film, not learning.
- **Adding Italian "because it's easy after Spanish".** It is easy after Spanish. Do it in July 2027.

# The free track

**Everything in this plan can be done for $0 except the exam fees.** B1 the *level* is free. B1 the *certificate* is not.

## What money actually buys

| Paid version | Free replacement | What you lose |
|---|---|---|
| **italki tutors** (~$150/mo) | HelloTalk, Tandem, [free4talk](https://free4talk.com) (24/7 drop-in voice rooms, no matching), Discord language servers, r/language_exchange, Brisbane Meetup conversation groups | 🔴 **Real.** No correction discipline, flaky partners, and roughly half your time is spent speaking English in return. Call it a 20–30% hit on the output engine. |
| **Dreaming Spanish** ($8/mo) | **Free tier: ~1,000 videos.** Premium adds ~4,000 more. | Basically nothing. 1,000 videos is far more than nine months of use. |
| **Kwiziq French** (~$20/mo) | [Lawless French](https://www.lawlessfrench.com), [Le Point du FLE](https://www.lepointdufle.net), Français avec Pierre's free grammar | Moderate. You lose the adaptive sequencing and — more importantly — your objective French progress metric. **Substitute:** free official DELF B1 sample papers, one section a month. |
| **Assimil** (~$45) | Language Transfer + free grammar sites, or borrow it from the library | Small. Assimil is a convenience, not a necessity. |
| **Graded readers** (~$50) | Brisbane City Council library (**BorrowBox / Libby** — free ebooks and audiobooks with a library card), QUT library's language collection, Project Gutenberg for public-domain Spanish and French, and podcast transcripts as reading material | Little, if the library stocks them. Check BCC's free language-platform access too — most Australian councils license one. |
| **Anki iOS** ($25) | **AnkiWeb in a phone browser** — free. Android app is free too. | Nothing but polish. |
| **Paid writing correction** | [LangCorrect](https://langcorrect.com), [Journaly](https://journaly.com), and **r/WriteStreakES** + **r/WriteStreakFR** — daily writing corrected free by natives | Nothing. Arguably better than paying a tutor to do it. |

## Your unfair advantage — use it

🔴 **You bartend in Brisbane.** Brisbane hospitality is full of Latin American and French backpackers. That's free, daily, high-volume speaking practice with native speakers *while you're being paid*. On sheer volume it beats italki — what it lacks is correction, which is exactly what the free writing-correction subreddits give you back.

Also worth twenty minutes of checking: QUT almost certainly runs a language-exchange or conversation-club program for its international students, and it's free to enrolled students.

## The verdict

Going fully free costs you maybe **10–15% of the outcome, concentrated entirely in speaking** — the one skill the exam scores separately and won't let you compensate for.

**If you can find $35/month**, buy one tutor session per language per fortnight. Not for the teaching — for the thing an exchange partner structurally cannot provide: someone whose job is to correct you, at a time you've already paid for and can't flake on. That single change closes most of the 10–15%.

**If you truly can't**, the free track works. It needs more discipline: schedule your exchange calls like appointments, always ask to be corrected, and lean hard on the writing subreddits to catch the errors nobody interrupts you for.
