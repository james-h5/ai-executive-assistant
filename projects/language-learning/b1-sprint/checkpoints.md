# Checkpoints & Tracking

> **Decided 2026-09-01: no exams.** James wants the *level*, not the certificate. That's a legitimate call — DELE and DELF measure a fairly narrow slice of ability and the exam-technique hours buy nothing you'd use in a bar in Lyon. It also makes the whole plan genuinely $0.
>
> But it removes the one irreversible commitment in the plan. Sections 1 and 2 below are what replace it. **Don't skip them** — they're doing the job the exam fee was doing.

---

## 1. What replaces the exam

An exam works as a deadline for two reasons: money already spent, and a date you don't control. Any substitute needs both.

### 🎯 Primary: book the pre-Sheffield trip

**Fly to Europe 3–4 weeks before Sheffield starts and spend it in Spain and France.**

This is strictly better than an exam for what you actually want:
- **Money spent, date fixed, not reschedulable** — same mechanism as an exam fee
- **The test is the real thing.** Not a role-play with an examiner — actual strangers, actual bars, actual not-understanding-the-waiter.
- **You'd want to do it anyway.** You're flying to the UK regardless; going three weeks early costs the accommodation, not the airfare.

**Action:** book flights as soon as Aug/Sept 2027 fares open (typically ~11 months ahead, so roughly **Oct 2026**). Book the first week of accommodation at the same time. 🔴 **The moment that's paid, "B1 by June" stops being negotiable** — it becomes "don't be the guy standing in a bar in Valencia nodding at things he doesn't understand."

### Secondary: the June recording

**1 June 2027**: record a 15-minute unscripted conversation in each language — with a tutor, an exchange partner, or a native mate. Put it next to the 60-second September recording.

Tell one person the date. That's the whole mechanism — a date and a witness.

### What you're giving up, honestly

Without the exam there's no external judge, and "B1" becomes something you can always claim you're *nearly* at. Section 2 is the antidote: tests that are **binary**. You can do the thing or you can't.

## 2. The B1 field tests — the real measurement

CEFR B1, in the Council of Europe's own words, is someone who *"can deal with most situations likely to arise whilst travelling"*, *"can describe experiences and events, dreams, hopes and ambitions"*, and *"can briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans."*

That's not an exam description. It's a description of exactly what you want. So test it directly.

**Run the full battery quarterly** — end of November, end of February, end of May — per language. Each is pass/fail. No partial credit, no "nearly".

### Listening
| # | Test | Pass = |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Watch 20 min of a native TV episode, **no subtitles**, unfamiliar episode | You can summarise the plot accurately, including *why* characters did things |
| L2 | Listen to one news bulletin (RFI / a Spanish news segment) **once** | You can state the three main stories |
| L3 | Have someone leave you a 60-second voicemail with practical details | You get the details right — no visual cues, no repeat |

L3 is the hardest and the most real. Phone audio with no face to read is where B1 listening actually gets tested.

### Speaking
| # | Test | Pass = |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | 30-minute conversation with a native, **zero English**, topics you didn't prepare | You never abandon a sentence. Slow and clumsy is fine; stopping dead isn't. |
| S2 | Explain something complex from your own life for 3 minutes, unprepared — futures trading, your degree, how a boxing match is scored | The listener follows it |
| S3 | **Handle a problem:** return a faulty item, change a booking, sort out a mistake on a bill | You resolve it without switching to English |
| S4 | Tell a story about something that happened to you, past tense throughout | Tenses are mostly right and the listener doesn't have to reconstruct the timeline |

**S3 is the single best proxy for B1.** It's literally the CEFR descriptor, and it's the thing that makes travel work.

### Reading
| # | Test | Pass = |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Read a newspaper article, no dictionary | You can explain it to someone afterwards |
| R2 | Read 10 pages of a YA novel, no dictionary | You follow the plot and enjoy it enough to keep going |

### Writing
| # | Test | Pass = |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | 180 words giving your opinion on something, 45 minutes | A native reads it without effort and without having to guess your meaning |

### Who judges

You can self-score L1–L3, R1–R2 honestly. **You cannot self-score speaking or writing.** Get a native to judge S1–S4 and W1 — a tutor, an exchange partner, or a Spanish/French backpacker at work. Show them the pass criteria above and ask them to be blunt.

**Free official DELF/DELE past papers are still worth using** — not to sit as exams, but as ready-made listening and reading material with an answer key. Free to download, and the answer key is an objective score when you want one.

## 2. Monthly checkpoints

Last Sunday of every month, 45 minutes. Log results in `progress/spanish.md` and `progress/french.md`.

| # | Test | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The recording** | 3-minute unscripted monologue, each language, on a random topic. Archive every one. Playing September back in May is the best motivation available. |
| 2 | **Kwiziq level %** | French especially. Objective, CEFR-mapped, no self-deception possible. |
| 3 | **Anki mature card count** | Words you actually retain, not words you've seen. |
| 4 | **Cold comprehension test** | One podcast episode you've never heard, no transcript. Estimate % understood. Same podcast series each month so it's comparable. |
| 5 | **Hours logged** | The number that predicts everything. |

### Monthly targets

| Month | Anki mature (each) | Cold comprehension | Kwiziq FR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sep | 300 | — | — |
| Oct | 600 | 15% | A1 done |
| Nov | 1,000 | 25% | A2 in progress |
| Dec | 1,500 | 40% | A2 done |
| Jan | 2,000 | 55% | B1 in progress |
| Feb | 2,400 | 65% | B1 ~50% |
| Mar | 2,700 | 75% | B1 ~70% |
| Apr | 3,000 | 80% | B1 ~85% |
| May | maintain | 85% | B1 ~90% |

~3,000 known words is roughly the B1 vocabulary threshold. That's the number to steer by.

**Three of these five are free and objective** — the recording, the Anki count, the hours. Cold comprehension is free and honest enough if you don't flatter yourself. Kwiziq is the only paid one; on the free track, drop that column and use a free DELF B1 sample paper's reading section instead, scored against its answer key.

**In November, February and May, the monthly checkpoint becomes the full field-test battery** (section 2). Budget half a day rather than 45 minutes — you need a native for the speaking tests.

## 3. Hour log

Log daily. One line. Do not skip this — the entire plan is a math bet and this is the only instrument reading it.

```
| Week | ES active | ES stacked | FR active | FR stacked | Total | Target | Notes |
|------|-----------|------------|-----------|------------|-------|--------|-------|
| W1 (1–7 Sep)   |  |  |  |  |  | 12 |  |
| W2 (8–14 Sep)  |  |  |  |  |  | 12 |  |
| W3 (15–21 Sep) |  |  |  |  |  | 12 |  |
| W4 (22–28 Sep) |  |  |  |  |  | 12 |  |
```

**Weekly targets:** Sept–Nov 12–13 · Dec–Feb 27 · Mar–May 18

**Running total to hit by 31 May: 750 hours.**

### The three-week rule
If your weekly total is under target three weeks running, **stop and recalibrate that week**. Not in May. The two honest options:

1. **Drop to one language.** Spanish only, at 750 hours, gets you to a comfortable B1+ or even B2. That's a better outcome than two shaky A2s.
2. **Move the target to September.** You don't fly out until August 2027 — that's three extra months of runway that a fixed exam date wouldn't have given you.

Both are fine. Grinding on while pretending the number will fix itself is not.

**Without an exam this rule matters more, not less.** It's now the only mechanism that catches a slide early, so treat "three weeks under" as a genuine stop-and-decide, not a note to yourself.

## 4. Abort / adjust criteria

| Trigger | Response |
|---|---|
| **Nov field tests: fewer than 4 of 10 passed** in a language | Normal at that stage — no action. This one is a baseline, not a gate. |
| **Feb field tests: S1 or S3 failed** in a language | That's the real warning sign. Give it 65% of March–April hours and double its speaking sessions. The other language goes to maintenance. |
| **3 weeks under target hours** | Recalibrate immediately — drop to one language, or push the target to September. |
| **Uni GPA drops below 5.5** | Uni wins. Zero-day minimums for a month. The degree is the actual asset and the exchange depends on it. |
| **May field tests: S1 and S3 both failed** in a language | Don't call it B1 — call it what it is and keep going. You've got June, July and August before you fly, plus immersion once you land. |

## 5. What "success" actually looks like

No certificate to point at, so define it concretely and don't let it drift:

**By 1 June 2027**, in both languages:
- ✅ **S1** — 30 minutes with a native, no English, unprepared topics, without a sentence dying in your mouth
- ✅ **S3** — you can sort out a problem: a wrong bill, a changed booking, a faulty item
- ✅ **L1** — a native TV episode, no subtitles, and you can say what happened and why

**By August 2027, in a bar in Valencia or Lyon:** an hour with a stranger — jokes, opinions, plans — without it being an ordeal for either of you.

**By September 2027:** you land in Sheffield with a year of cheap flights ahead and nothing linguistic standing between you and any of it.

That last one was always the actual goal. The diplomas were only ever a way of proving to yourself you'd got there — and if you'd rather prove it in a bar than in an exam hall, that's the better test anyway.
