# B1 Sprint — Spanish + French by June 2027

**The goal:** B1 (CEFR) ability in Spanish *and* French — the level, not the certificate. **No exams** (decided 2026-09-01).

**The why:** Sheffield exchange starts Sept 2027 — a full year in the UK with budget flights to France and Spain every other weekend. Landing in Europe already conversational in both is the difference between a year of tourism and a year of actually meeting people.

**The deadline:** with no exam to book, the forcing function is a **flight**. Fly to Europe three weeks early, August 2027, and spend it in Spain and France. Book it in October 2026 when fares open. Money spent, date fixed, and the test is the real thing rather than a role-play with an examiner.

**How you'll know you got there:** ten binary field tests per language, run quarterly — see `checkpoints.md` §2. No score to round up, no "nearly".

## 1. The honest math

Cambridge puts B1 at **350–400 guided learning hours** from zero. Two languages = **~750 hours** in 39 weeks (1 Sept 2026 → 31 May 2027).

That's **~19 hours a week. ~2h45 a day. Every day.**

That number is only survivable because of one distinction:

| Hour type | What it is | Cost to your day |
|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Desk work — grammar, Anki, writing, tutor calls, focused reading | Expensive. Competes directly with uni. |
| **Stacked** | Listening during commute, gym, cooking, walking, bar prep, dishes | Nearly free. Uses dead time you already have. |

The plan runs **~1h15 active + ~1h30 stacked** on a normal day. Only the active hour competes with uni. The stacked hour is time you're currently spending on music and podcasts in English.

### Where the plan lives or dies

**Phase 2 — the summer — is 47% of your total hours.** Sem 2 ends mid-Nov; Summer 2026/27 is only MGB130 + EFB210 (24cp, vs the 60cp you're carrying now). December and January are the closest thing to a free run you'll get before Sheffield.

Blow the summer and B1 by June is over. Not "harder" — over. There is no later window: Sem 1 2027 is a full 48cp and its exam block lands in June.

## 2. Method — the four engines

Every session belongs to one of these. If an activity doesn't fit one, cut it.

### Engine 1 — Structure (the machinery)
Understand *how the language assembles* before drowning in vocab. **Language Transfer** first, for both, in the first six weeks — free, audio-only, and it teaches the mechanism rather than phrases. Then targeted grammar (Kwiziq) to fill gaps the input exposes, never grammar for its own sake.

### Engine 2 — Input (the volume)
Comprehensible input, level-matched, in huge quantity. This is where all the stacked hours go and it's the single biggest driver of listening comprehension — the skill that decides whether B1 feels real or fake.

### Engine 3 — Output (the terror)
Speaking with a human from **week 5** (Spanish) and **week 9** (French). Non-negotiable and deliberately early.

### Engine 4 — Consolidation (the ratchet)
Anki daily so nothing leaks out, plus writing from Month 3 to force precision your speaking lets you dodge.

### The two asymmetries

These languages have *different bottlenecks*. Treating them identically wastes months.

- **French's bottleneck is listening.** Spoken French deviates hard from written French — liaison, elision, dropped syllables. You will read French comfortably long before you can hear it. → French gets **more listening hours, dictation drills, and a Week 1 pronunciation boot camp.**
- **Spanish's bottleneck is verb production.** The phonetics are clean and you'll understand it fast; what breaks is generating the right conjugation live. → Spanish gets **more speaking volume and conjugation drilling**, and starts tutor sessions four weeks earlier.

### Rejecting the Dreaming Spanish "silent period"

Dreaming Spanish tells you not to speak until ~600 hours of input, and not to read until ~600. **Ignore that here.** It's excellent advice for a different goal — near-native accent and effortless fluency over 2–3 years. You have 9 months and an exam with a 25-point speaking section. Use their videos (they're the best input library going) and discard their timeline.

## 3. Handling two Romance languages at once

Interference is real and will happen. It mostly stops mattering past A2. Mitigations:

1. **Hard time separation.** Spanish is always morning. French is always afternoon/evening. Never within 30 minutes of each other, ever.
2. **Separate everything.** Separate Anki decks, separate notebooks, separate physical spots if you can.
3. **Never translate between them.** Both route through English or through images — never Spanish↔French.
4. **Different voices.** Anchor each language to distinct hosts so your ear tags them by sound, not by grammar.
5. **When you mix them up, don't panic.** Note it, move on. It's a phase, not a failure signal.

## 4. The rules

- **The zero-day rule.** Minimum viable day = 10 min Anki + 20 min listening, *each language*. 60 minutes total. On your worst day, with a hangover, after a double shift — you still hit 60 minutes. Streak integrity matters more than any single big day.
- **Track every hour.** The whole plan is a math bet. Log it (see `checkpoints.md`). If your weekly total is under 15 hours three weeks running, the June target is gone — recalibrate *then*, in the moment, not in May. **With no exam date, this is now the only early-warning system you have.** Treat it as a real stop-and-decide.
- **No third language.** Italian, Portuguese and German are parked until June 2027. Don't touch them. (`schedule.md` rotation is suspended.)
- **No resource-hopping.** Pick from the stack in `resources.md` and stay with it for the whole phase. Switching apps feels like progress and isn't.
- **Speak before you're ready.** Always. You will never feel ready.
- **Uni comes first when they collide.** During the Nov and June exam blocks, drop to zero-day minimums without guilt. That's budgeted for.

## 6. Cost — three ways to run this

**Everything here can be done for $0 except the exam fees.** B1 the *level* is free. B1 the *certificate* is not. Full breakdown of what money buys and what free costs you: `resources.md` → **The free track**.

| Track | Monthly | What you give up |
|---|---|---|
| **Full** | ~$180 | Nothing. italki 4×/wk, Dreaming Spanish premium, Kwiziq French. |
| **🎯 Hybrid — recommended** | **~$35** | Almost nothing. Everything free, plus one tutor session per language per fortnight. |
| **Free** | **$0** | ~10–15% of the outcome, all of it in speaking. Workable, needs more discipline. |

**With the exams dropped, the free track is now genuinely $0.** The ~$650–750 in exam fees was the only unavoidable cost in the whole plan, and it's gone.

**Why the hybrid is the recommendation:** the one thing a free language exchange structurally cannot give you is someone whose job is to correct you, at a time you've already paid for and can't flake on. Two sessions a month buys that back for the price of a couple of beers.

**And your unfair advantage:** you bartend in Brisbane, which is full of Latin American and French backpackers. That's free daily speaking practice with natives while you're being paid. On volume it beats italki — it just can't correct you, which is what the free writing-correction subreddits are for.

## 8. Reality check

This is achievable and it is not comfortable. The failure mode is not "too hard" — it's a quiet two-week slide in October where you tell yourself you'll catch up in the summer, and then the summer gets eaten by something else.

The last language project logged zero sessions across two months. That's the pattern to beat.

**Dropping the exams removes the strongest defence against that pattern**, so the replacements have to be taken seriously rather than nodded at: the booked flight, the hour log, and the 1 June recording with someone expecting it. An exam is a stranger with a clipboard telling you what you are. Without one, nothing stops "B1" quietly becoming a thing you're always *nearly* at — except tests you can't argue with. That's what the field-test battery is for.
