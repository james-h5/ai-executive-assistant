# Portuguese Primer

*European Portuguese (Portugal) — the version below. Brazilian Portuguese is close but has some spelling/pronunciation differences (e.g. "dezesseis" instead of "dezasseis" for 16); worth a note if you end up in Brazil instead.*

## Pronunciation
- Closest sound-alike to Spanish, but softer/more mumbled — European Portuguese often drops vowel sounds Spanish keeps
- Nasal vowels: "ão" (like "ow" through the nose), "ãe"
- `s` at the end of a word often sounds like "sh" (Portuguese-specific quirk vs. Spanish/Italian)
- `lh` = "ly" sound, `nh` = "ny" sound
- `x` varies — can be "sh", "z", or "ks" depending on the word

## Numbers 1–20
um, dois, três, quatro, cinco, seis, sete, oito, nove, dez, onze, doze, treze, catorze, quinze, dezasseis, dezassete, dezoito, dezanove, vinte

## Core Grammar
- Nouns are gendered: masculine uses "o", feminine uses "a" (o livro, a mesa)
- Plurals: add "-s"
- Verbs conjugate by pronoun — three families: -ar, -er, -ir (very similar structure to Spanish, different sounds)
- "Obrigado" (said by men) / "Obrigada" (said by women) for "thank you" — matches the speaker's own gender, not the listener's
