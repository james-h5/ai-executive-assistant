# Political Literacy

Build the habit of forming political opinions from primary sources and actual track records — not from outrage clips, TikTok soundbites, or whoever posted loudest.

**Status:** Active

## Description

Triggered by the school/uni walkouts protesting Pauline Hanson's One Nation (Aug 2026) — James's read was that a lot of the protesters were reacting to a viral clip or an old stunt, not to anything in One Nation's actual policy platform. This project is the fix: a repeatable method for evaluating any party or politician on what they actually say and do, plus a living comparison doc (`party-comparison.md`) covering the major Australian parties on the policy areas that matter most to James.

This is a personal-development workstream, not income-generating — same category as `language-learning/` and `communication-excellence/`.

## The Method

1. **Separate the person from the policy.** Character/optics (how someone comes across, a clip, a stunt) and policy substance (what they'd actually legislate) are different questions. Both are legitimate to weigh, but don't let one answer the other.
2. **Go to primary sources, not commentary.**
   - Party platforms (official party websites)
   - [They Vote For You](https://theyvoteforyou.org.au) — non-partisan tracker of every MP/Senator's actual votes
   - [Hansard](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Hansard) — the verbatim parliamentary record
   - [Parliamentary Library research papers](https://www.aph.gov.au/About_Parliament/Parliamentary_Departments/Parliamentary_Library/pubs/rp) — non-partisan policy backgrounders
   - [ABC Vote Compass](https://www.abc.net.au/news/elections/vote-compass) — shows where you sit vs. each party by policy, not by vibe
   - [AEC](https://www.aec.gov.au) — electoral basics, seat counts, results
3. **For each policy area, separate four things:**
   - What they *say* (platform)
   - What they've actually *done* — voted for/against when it counted (track record beats rhetoric)
   - Who benefits, who's worse off — follow the incentive
   - The steelman — the strongest honest version of their argument, from someone who actually believes it, not the version their opponents would give you
4. **Pressure-test your gut.** Deliberately read/watch a source that leans the opposite way of your instinct on at least one issue before locking in a view.
5. **Spot outrage-bait before it shapes your opinion.** Out-of-context clips, a headline that doesn't match the actual quote, anonymous reposts with no source — if a claim gives you a strong emotional reaction, that's the signal to go verify it, not to share it.
6. **Weigh feasibility, not just intent.** A minor party's manifesto isn't the same as a governing party's — check seat counts / Senate balance and constitutional limits before assuming a policy would actually happen if they got power.

## Working Doc

See `party-comparison.md` — structured policy area → party → (what they say / track record / trade-offs), each claim tagged with its source. Priority areas: Education & HECS, Immigration & migration, Tax & small business/trading, plus a broader economy/cost-of-living/energy overview.

## Review Cadence

Revisit and refresh sourced claims whenever a new issue prompts it, or ahead of the next federal election cycle — party platforms and voting records shift between elections.
