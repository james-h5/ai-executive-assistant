# Mechanics Checklist

The full curriculum broken into individual, checkable behaviors. A mechanic is something you can catch yourself doing or not doing in the moment — "composure" is not a mechanic, "pause two seconds before answering" is.

**How to use:** one mechanic per week, not the whole list. Drill it live on bar shifts (4-5 nights a week of stranger interactions is the training ground). Debrief with `/monte-cristo`.

---

## Tier 0 — Confirmed Live Weak Points (start here)

These are the four surfaced in real sessions, logged in `progress.md`. Everything else is theory until these are closed.

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | **Cut the stakes-linked filler** — "um," "you know," "yeah" lead-ins. Escalates with senior/appraising people specifically | Cut the lead-in entirely, deliver flat. Catch it live, not in hindsight |
| 0.2 | **Stop minimizing what you do** — "not much," "nothing exciting" | State it plainly and stop. Double degree in engineering and finance is not "not much" |
| 0.3 | **Anchor to the room when the words won't come** | Name the actual object or event in front of you rather than reaching for a new idea from nothing |
| 0.4 | **The second window** — a parting remark is still live | Any signal, first or second, gets the same reset. "Already missed it" is not a reason to miss the next one |

## Tier 1 — Nervous System (foundation; everything above this fails without it)

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Physiological sigh on trigger** | Two short inhales through the nose, one long exhale through the mouth. Under 5 seconds, invisible |
| 1.2 | **Shoulder drop + jaw unclench** | Tension check on any spike, release both immediately |
| 1.3 | **Breath → body → words, in that order** | Move physically (half-step, body turn, held eye contact) before searching for the line. Motion breaks freeze; a perfect sentence doesn't |
| 1.4 | **Box breathing pre-load** | 4 in, 4 hold, 4 out, 4 hold — 60 seconds before any room with stakes |
| 1.5 | **Lower the stakes on purpose** | Goal of any single interaction is "be present for 10 seconds," not "win" |

## Tier 2 — Voice

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Chest voice, not throat** | Hand on chest — it should vibrate slightly when you speak |
| 2.2 | **Kill uptalk** | End statements flat or falling, never rising |
| 2.3 | **Half-beat pause before responding** | Reads as thoughtful, not slow. Idris Elba leaves 2-3 seconds |
| 2.4 | **Pause instead of filling** | Silence is not a gap that needs patching |
| 2.5 | **Match the room's volume** | Talking quietly enough to half-hope you're not heard reads as uncertainty |
| 2.6 | **Deliver, then stop** | Don't stack three sentences to fill space while waiting for a response |
| 2.7 | **Tone stays level when stakes rise** | Pitch/speed rising the moment stakes appear is the tell that gives away nerves faster than the words |

---

## Tier 3 — Body

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Shoulders back and *down*, chest open** | Up around the ears is tension, not confidence. Caved chest is the freeze tell |
| 3.2 | **Walk unhurried, arms swinging, eyes up** | Eyes down means the opportunity is invisible before you can act on it |
| 3.3 | **Settled stance, no rocking or weight-shifting** | Reads as impatience or nerves |
| 3.4 | **Hands visible, no self-touch fidgeting** | Face-touching, hair, adjusting clothes — most visible tell there is |
| 3.5 | **Sit back, don't perch** | Perching on the edge reads as ready to flee |
| 3.6 | **Eye contact through the end of your sentence** | Break naturally, not darting away mid-thought |
| 3.7 | **Stillness — cut extraneous movement** | The Brando/Zidane mechanic. Magnetism comes from *not* moving unnecessarily. Stillness ≠ stiffness — hands still gesture to support speech |

---

## Tier 4 — Face and Frame

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **Resting face neutral-*relaxed*, not neutral-flat** | Softened brow and mouth. A tense inward face can't notice signals |
| 4.2 | **Genuine smile — eyes engaged** | Slight crinkle at the eyes. Practicable in a mirror, very visible difference |
| 4.3 | **Eyebrow flash on greeting** | Quick natural raise — universally read as friendly recognition |
| 4.4 | **Kill the flinch face** | Eyes widening / slight recoil when someone unexpected engages reads as rejection |
| 4.5 | **Hold frame after a miss** | No slumping, no visible deflation, no over-explaining. A held frame after a miss beats a perfect opener followed by collapse |

---

## Tier 5 — Conversation

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Situational openers, never appearance compliments** | Compliments signal intent immediately and put people on guard |
| 5.2 | **Specific over abstract** | "Best thing you've eaten this week" beats "what do you do for fun" |
| 5.3 | **Genuine follow-up, not your prepared next topic** | This is what makes conversation feel alive instead of like an interview |
| 5.4 | **Trade information — don't interview** | Answer your own question briefly after they do, or offer something first |
| 5.5 | **Story: setup → turn → payoff, then stop** | Cut throat-clearing detail. Don't explain the punchline |
| 5.6 | **Banter as light challenge, not insult** | "You say that like it's a good thing" |
| 5.7 | **Callback humor** | Reference something from earlier — reads as attentive without needing quick wit |
| 5.8 | **Listen without pre-planning your reply** | People remember how the conversation felt, not your lines |
| 5.9 | **Escalate directly, no vague trail-off** | "I'd like to keep talking to you, can I get your number" beats "we should hang out sometime" |

---

## Tier 6 — Reading Signals

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **Match, then slightly escalate** | Match the signal's level first — proves it was noticed without overcommitting |
| 6.2 | **Don't flinch on touch** | Same reset (sigh, drop shoulders). Going rigid reads as rejection even when it's surprise |
| 6.3 | **Read disinterest and de-escalate gracefully** | Information to act on, not a failure to fix |
| 6.4 | **Test ambiguity with a low-cost escalation** | The response tells you more than guessing would |

---

## Tier 7 — Conviction and Authority

Added 2026-09-09 from the traits James named: Mourinho (self-belief), Obama (composure in disagreement), Idris Elba (calm authority / room adjusts to his pace).

| # | Mechanic | Drill |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | **Cut every hedge** | Delete "I think," "kind of," "maybe," "hopefully," "sort of," "probably" from spoken statements. Mourinho never qualifies — he states. This is the single biggest lever on sounding certain |
| 7.2 | **Say the outcome as fact, casually** | Not "I'm hoping to get to Europe next year" — "I'm going to Europe next year." Same information, completely different signal |
| 7.3 | **Acknowledge specifically before you counter** | The Obama mechanic. Restate their actual point in your own words *first*, then disagree. Makes disagreement feel respectful without conceding anything |
| 7.4 | **Never raise volume in disagreement** | Drop it slightly instead. Volume escalation is the loss of frame |
| 7.5 | **Disagree with the idea, not the person** | "I don't think that holds up because —" not "you're wrong" |
| 7.6 | **Don't let another person set your pace** | If they're fast and urgent, stay at your speed. The room adjusting to you *is* the mechanic — it's not a byproduct |
| 7.7 | **Answer the question asked, then stop** | Over-explaining and justifying is the most common way certainty leaks out of an otherwise good answer |

## Suggested Sequence

Twelve weeks, one mechanic at a time, in the order that unblocks the most:

1. **0.1** — cut filler (most visible, already confirmed live)
2. **0.2** — stop minimizing
3. **7.1** — cut hedges (pairs directly with 0.2, same underlying tell)
4. **2.3** — half-beat pause (makes 0.1 and 7.1 easier — filler is what fills the gap)
5. **3.6** — eye contact through end of sentence
6. **2.2** — kill uptalk
7. **3.7** — stillness
8. **4.2** — genuine smile, eyes engaged
9. **5.3** — genuine follow-up questions
10. **7.3** — acknowledge before countering
11. **7.6** — hold your own pace
12. **5.9** — direct escalation

Then re-run the list from the top — the early ones will have decayed and the later ones get easier the second pass.

## Note on What Mechanics Can't Do

Delivery is roughly 20% of this. The other 80% is having something to say, which comes from a life worth talking about and genuine curiosity about other people. The Europe trip, the three languages, boxing, Japan — that's the raw material. Drilling mechanics on an empty story bank produces someone polished and hollow, which people read instantly. See `story-bank.md`.
