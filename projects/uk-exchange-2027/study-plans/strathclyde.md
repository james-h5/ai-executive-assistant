# University of Strathclyde — Candidate Units (3rd preference)

Chosen as 3rd preference on the theory that Scotland isn't "England" for QUT's "USA or England limited to 1st/2nd preference only" rule — confirmed correct by James selecting it. Strathclyde has one of the strongest dedicated Electronic & Electrical Engineering departments in Scotland.

**Source: real, detailed exchange class catalogue** — `strath.ac.uk/studywithus/studyabroad/comingtostrathclyde/academicinformation/exchangeclasscatalogue/electronicelectricalengineering/` — comparable in quality to Sheffield's, better than Aston's (which had no public per-module descriptions at all). Full descriptions, credits, semester timing, and prerequisites all published.

**Structure:** exchange students choose classes from Levels 1–3 (Level 4 mentioned as the norm for home students' final year, not confirmed open to exchange). 60 Strathclyde credits (30 ECTS) per semester = 120/year. Many classes offer both a 10cr semester-only version and a 20cr full-year version.

## Level 4 options (ideal content, access unconfirmed)

- **EE467** Power System Design, Operation and Protection (20cr, Full Year) — would be the best possible EGB341 match (generation/transmission/distribution, fault analysis, protection schemes) if Level 4 access is confirmed for exchange students
- **EE472** Control Principles (20cr, Full Year) — closer to EGB345's actual control-theory content than EE312, same access caveat

Worth asking Strathclyde's exchange team (`incoming.exchanges@strath.ac.uk` — not James's preferred contact method right now, but worth knowing it exists) whether Level 4 is genuinely closed to exchange students or just uncommon, since these two would meaningfully strengthen the two units that have been weakest across every university researched so far (EGB341 in particular).

## Status

Lower priority than Sheffield/Aston — QUT only assesses 1st/2nd preference study plans before nomination; Strathclyde's plan only gets assessed if both fall through. This list exists mainly to satisfy the application's "at least one unit" requirement, but ended up being a genuinely strong option — arguably the cleanest EGB241 solution of the three universities researched.
