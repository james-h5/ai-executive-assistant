# University of Birmingham — Candidate Units (Pass 1)

Engineering confirmed accessible for single-semester exchange (unlike Chemical Engineering, which is explicitly full-year-only — Electrical/Electronic wasn't listed as excluded). One logistics note: single-semester Engineering students may need to sit summative assessment in the **January or May/June** assessment period at Birmingham, or have it invigilated at the home university (QUT) — not a blocker, just needs coordinating. Business School has no single-semester restriction noted, only an English-language threshold (IELTS 6.5, etc. — shouldn't be an issue).

Sources: [Academic modules for Incoming exchange programme](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/study/international/study-abroad-exchange/exchange-programme/academic-modules), [Electronic and Electrical Engineering BEng](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/subjects/electronic-electrical-and-systems-engineering-courses/electronic-electrical-engineering-beng), [Accounting and Finance BSc](https://www.birmingham.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/subjects/accounting-and-finance-courses/accounting-and-finance-bsc)

## Read on the leftover-unit question
Birmingham's core gap mirrors Manchester Met's: **EGB345 (the critical semester-locked unit) is bundled into a combined "Electrical Energy Systems and Control" module rather than getting a standalone match**, and no dedicated Year 3 control-systems module surfaced in this pass. That's a real open question — either the bundled Year 2 module is deep enough alone (needs the actual syllabus to judge), or Birmingham has the same weak spot as Bristol/Leeds on this specific unit. The optional **Power Electronics and Power Systems** module is a genuine win for EGB341 though — cleaner than the equivalent bundled/partial options found at Aston, Bristol, and Sheffield's Year 2 option.
