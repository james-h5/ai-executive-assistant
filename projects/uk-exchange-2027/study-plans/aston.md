# Aston University — Candidate Units (2nd preference)

## 2026-08-27: Re-scoped for engineering-only, full year

Previous research below (kept for its accessibility/structural findings) was built against the old 9-unit target (5 fixed 2027 S2 units + 4 swappable finance/business units). James has since chosen a **full-year, engineering-only** exchange (see `sheffield.md`), so Aston — as a candidate 2nd preference alongside Sheffield — needs matching against the current 6-unit target instead: EGB240, EGB241, EGB348, EGB341, EGB342, EGB345. None of the finance/business matches below (EFB335, EFB343, EFB344, BSB399) are relevant anymore under this choice.

**Confirmed directly from Aston's incoming-exchange page:** the Year 1/2-only restriction applies to the School of Life & Health Sciences specifically — **Engineering and Physical Sciences has no stated year-level restriction**, so Year 3 ("Final Year") content is plausible for exchange, though not explicitly confirmed as open (see caveats below).

**Structural difference from Sheffield, worth flagging before treating Aston as a like-for-like backup:** Aston's modules are uniformly 15 credits each (unlike Sheffield's mix of 10/20/30cr). A full year = 120 Aston credits (60 ECTS) = **8 modules**, not 6. Since James only has 6 units' worth of real content to match, an Aston-based plan would need **2 extra "top-up" units** (per QUT's own guidance: academic content, assessed, no QUT credit — just there to satisfy full-time enrolment volume) to reach 120 credits. This is a real cost of choosing Aston over Sheffield, where 6 year-long 20cr modules cleanly summed to a full load.

**Confidence level — lower than Sheffield's verified pass:** this is built from Aston's public course-structure page (real module names/credits, fetched 2026-08-27), not an exchange-specific module catalogue with subject/university-wide access flags like Sheffield's spreadsheet. Aston didn't publish one in this pass — `eas_exchange@aston.ac.uk` (found on the incoming-exchange page) is the direct contact to confirm the real exchange-accessible list and whether Final Year specialisation streams are genuinely open to exchange students.

Source: [MEng Electrical and Electronic Engineering](https://www.aston.ac.uk/study/courses/electrical-and-electronic-engineering-meng), [Incoming Exchange Students](https://www.aston.ac.uk/international/erasmus/incoming-exchange)

### Same two weak spots as Sheffield, different unit affected

Sheffield's weak spot was also EGB241 (no single module covers both electromagnetics *and* machines) — Aston has the same gap from the other direction (machines only, no fields module at all). EGB341 has literally no candidate at either university researched so far. This is starting to look like a genuine hole in what a standard UK EEE undergraduate exchange offers, not a university-specific quirk — worth lowering expectations that any UK partner solves it cleanly, and planning for EGB341 to possibly need bringing home to QUT regardless of which university is chosen.
