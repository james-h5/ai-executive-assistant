# Manchester Metropolitan University — Candidate Units (Pass 1)

Exchange guidance is vaguer here than the other five: "exchanges usually last at least a semester, but more often the whole academic year" — not a hard restriction like Leeds, but less explicit confirmation than Bristol/Sheffield/Aston/City that single-semester (Sept–Jan) is a clean, well-supported option. Guidance also recommends sticking to a **maximum of two departments** to avoid timetable clashes — workable since our units span Engineering + Business anyway, but worth keeping the mix tight.

No semester (autumn/spring) breakdown was available for any module found in this pass — bigger open item here than for Bristol/Sheffield, needs direct follow-up.

Sources: [BEng Electrical and Electronic Engineering](https://www.mmu.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/course/beng-electrical-and-electronic-engineering/september-2026), [BSc Banking and Finance](https://www.mmu.ac.uk/study/undergraduate/course/bsc-banking-and-finance), [Choose your modules (exchange)](https://www.mmu.ac.uk/study/international/exchange-to-manchester-met/choose-your-modules)

## Against EGB345 — Control and Dynamic Systems
- **Power and Control Engineering** (Year 2) / **Advanced Power And Control** (Year 3) — control content is bundled together with power/energy content in one module rather than split out like QUT does. Plausible match but the combined scope means less depth on control specifically than EGB345 alone — needs the actual syllabus to judge properly.

## Against EGB341 — Energy Supply and Delivery
- Same two modules as above (**Power and Control Engineering** / **Advanced Power And Control**) — power/energy content is bundled with control here, unclear how much is genuinely supply/transmission/delivery-focused vs. general power engineering

## Against EGB348 — Electronics
- **Electronic Circuits** (Year 2) / **Electronic Design** (Year 3) — plausible match, need syllabus detail to confirm semiconductor-device focus

## Against EGB342 — Telecommunications and RF
- **Communications and Business** (Year 3) — unusual combined title; unclear how much is genuine telecom/RF content vs. business-communications framing. **Needs the syllabus before trusting this as a real match** — could turn out to be the wrong kind of "communications" entirely.

## Against EFB335 — Investments
- Financial Analysis for Investment Banking (Year 3) — decent match, though less precisely named than similar modules found at Aston/Bristol/City

## Against EFB344 — Risk Management and Derivatives
- **Applied Risk Management and External Regulation in Banking** (Year 2) and **Advanced Risk Management** (Year 3) — strong, well-named match

## Against EFB343 — Corporate Finance
- No dedicated corporate finance module surfaced in this pass (may exist as an elective not shown on the public course page) — open item

## Against BSB399, EGH404
No matches found — consistent with every other university checked.

## Read on the leftover-unit question
Genuinely unclear without syllabus detail. The finance side has a clear risk-management strength (better named than most other universities for EFB344) but a Corporate Finance gap. The engineering side is the bigger question mark — "Power and Control Engineering" bundling both EGB345 and EGB341 content into one module could mean either a good economical fit or too shallow on both — and "Communications and Business" needs verifying it's actually about telecom/RF before counting on it for EGB342.
