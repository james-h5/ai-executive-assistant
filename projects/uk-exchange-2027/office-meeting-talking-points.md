# Talking Points — Study Abroad Office Meeting

## Why am I doing exchange?

**1. It's the whole point of the degree, for James specifically.** His stated top priority (`context/me.md`) is experiencing as much of the world as possible — not a throwaway line, it's the reason the AI consulting + trading income push exists at all (fund a location-independent life). He's already backpacked Japan solo for 2 months and wants more extended trips like it. A full year in the UK, bookended by backpacking, is that priority acted on rather than deferred until "after uni."

**2. The academic fit is real, not just an excuse to travel.** Every one of the 6 engineering units he has left to take before his advanced electives (Electronic Design, Electromagnetics and Machines, Electronics, Energy Supply and Delivery, Telecommunications and RF, Control and Dynamic Systems) has a matched Sheffield module, verified against Sheffield's actual published 2026/27 exchange catalogue — not just a course title that sounds similar. This wasn't a "find something, anything" search; specific modules were compared line-by-line against QUT's unit content (see `study-plans/sheffield.md`).

**3. A full year, not a semester, because the content match is actually stronger that way.** He originally planned a single semester, but under that constraint two of his six units (Electronic Design and Electromagnetics and Machines) had no workable slot — they were still scheduled at QUT either side of the exchange. Going full year and choosing the engineering-only (subject-exchange) track means all six get taught at a UK institution with a genuinely strong Electrical and Electronic Engineering school, instead of splitting the degree across two institutions for units that were easy to line up and leaving the harder-to-match ones behind.

**4. UK exposure is directly relevant to his major, not just "study abroad" for its own sake.** Power systems, control engineering, and telecommunications are taught with different standards, conventions, and industry context in the UK than in Australia. Seeing both before he graduates is a genuine professional-development angle he can speak to, not just a personal one.

**5. Logistics already de-risked.** He holds a British passport, so there's no visa application, no work restriction, no Immigration Health Surcharge — the practical barriers that make a full year abroad harder for most students don't apply to him. That's part of why full year became viable at all (see `decisions/log.md`, 2026-08-03 entry for the reasoning history).
