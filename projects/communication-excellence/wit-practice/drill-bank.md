# Wit Practice — Drill Bank

Reference bank for the `wit-practice` skill. Pull from here each session, rotating so nothing repeats two sessions in a row (check `progress.md` for what was last used).

## Round 1 — Quick-Fire Categories

**Word Association** — throw a random word, James free-associates the funniest angle on it, fast.
Examples: "toaster", "Monday", "gym membership", "HECS debt", "self-checkout"

**Roast This Situation** — describe a mundane scenario, James roasts it in one line.
Examples: a guy taking a mirror selfie at the gym, someone microwaving fish in a shared kitchen, a LinkedIn "grateful and humbled" post, a bar customer who sends a drink back three times

**Absurd Hypothetical** — a ridiculous "what if," James answers in character, committed and quick.
Examples: "You're the world's worst pilot — reassure the passengers", "You're a boxing coach for a team of toddlers", "You have to sell someone a broken umbrella"

**Comeback to a Jab** — Claude delivers a light jab at James (never mean, always deflectable), he claps back.
Examples: "Bartender who can't make a decent espresso martini", "Tutor who probably can't do his own tax return", "3-3 record and still talks like he's undefeated"

**Caption This** — Claude describes an image/scene in one sentence, James writes the caption.
Examples: a cat knocking a glass off a table mid-eye-contact, a guy asleep at his desk with 47 browser tabs open, a dog wearing sunglasses guarding a esky at the beach

## Round 2 — Real-Life Fallback Situations
Use these when James has nothing fresh from his week.

- A regular at the bar orders something absurdly complicated during a rush
- A tutoring student says "I don't get it" for the fifth time on the same problem
- Someone at the boxing gym talks big before sparring and then gasses out in round 1
- A mate cancels plans last-minute with an obviously fake excuse
- A client on a discovery call asks "so what even is AI, like actually"

## Round 3 — Live Scenario Settings
Rotate context each session — don't repeat the same one twice running.

**Bar** — Claude plays a customer (regular, first-timer, mildly drunk, big tipper, complainer). Banter over the bar, keep it moving.

**Boxing Gym** — Claude plays a training partner or coach. Pre-sparring trash talk, post-round banter, someone psyching themselves up too hard.

**Tutoring** — Claude plays a student or a parent. Light banter that builds rapport without derailing the lesson.

**Consulting/Client** — Claude plays a small-business owner on a call, a bit skeptical of AI, testing if James is worth trusting. Wit here should build credibility, not just land jokes.

**General Social** — Claude plays a stranger at a party, a date, or a mate ribbing him. Freest-form round, most room to be sharp.
